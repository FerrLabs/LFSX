# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to the people you are working with. Argue with the code, not with the person who wrote it.

## What is expected

- **Criticise the work, not its author.** "This drops the lock when the store times out" is a
  review. "You clearly did not think about this" is not, and it is also less useful, because it
  says nothing about what to change.
- **Assume the other person had a reason.** Most surprising code is a constraint you have not met
  yet. Ask what it was before deciding it was carelessness.
- **Take disagreement in good faith.** A maintainer refusing a feature is not rejecting you; this
  project says no to things on purpose, and the reason will be given.
- **Respect the time people give.** Reports with the version and the logs, patches with tests, and
  answers to review questions are all ways of doing that.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment in any form, including sexual attention, slurs, and comments demeaning to someone's
identity or background. Personal attacks. Publishing anyone's private information. Sustained
disruption of discussions, which includes reopening a settled decision for the fifth time.

None of this depends on whether you meant it as a joke, and none of it becomes acceptable because a
patch is good.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, code review, discussions, and any space where somebody is acting as part of
this project.

## Reporting

Email <contact@ferrlabs.com> with `CONDUCT` in the subject. Reports are read by the maintainers and
kept confidential, including from the person reported, unless you agree otherwise. If your report
concerns a maintainer, say so and it will be handled by someone else.

Expect an acknowledgement within 3 working days. The response depends on what happened, from a
private word to a permanent ban, and you will be told what was decided.
