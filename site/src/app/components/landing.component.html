Your assets. Your disk.

LFSX stores the large binaries of a Git repository, game assets, textures, models and video, so they never touch your host's LFS quota. Your repository stays on GitHub, GitLab, Gitea or Forgejo. Only the LFS transfer is redirected to your own server.

A 3 GB asset pack cloned by a CI job ten times a month is 30 GB of metered traffic.

GitHub bills LFS storage and bandwidth separately from your plan, and a Unity or Unreal project burns through the free tier in a single push. Self-hosting removes the meter entirely: the cost becomes a disk you already own.

No accounts to manage

A client presents the token it would use to clone over HTTPS, and LFSX asks the forge what that token is allowed to do. There is no user table, no invitations, and nothing to revoke twice.
